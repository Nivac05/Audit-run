E1. BUG -- line.split(' ') instead of line.split()
============================================================================================
  eng line 7: split(' ')->8 words, split()->7 words  (1 phantom empty string)
     'Please keep the books  in the cupboard.'
  hin line 10: split(' ')->6 words, split()->5 words  (1 phantom empty string)
     'किताबें  अलमारी में रखी हैं।'
  v0 (buggy)                                   eng  1.265  hin  7.448  ratio  5.89x
  fixed: split()                               eng  1.283  hin  7.598  ratio  5.92x   [ratio  +0.6% vs v0]
  DIRECTION: phantom words inflate the denominator, so v0 UNDERSTATES fertility
  for any language whose corpus contains double spaces. Both corpora contain one,
  so it partially cancels in the ratio -- which is luck, not design.

============================================================================================
E2. BUG (the important one) -- line.lower() is not script-symmetric
============================================================================================
  eng: tokens cased=96 lowered=99 delta=+3 (+3.1%)  lines altered by .lower() = 10/10
  hin: tokens cased=459 lowered=459 delta=+0 (+0.0%)  lines altered by .lower() = 0/10
  v0 (lowercased)                              eng  1.265  hin  7.448  ratio  5.89x
  fixed: preserve case                         eng  1.229  hin  7.448  ratio  6.06x   [ratio  +2.9% vs v0]
  MECHANISM: Devanagari is unicameral -- .lower() is a literal no-op on it (0/10
  lines change). On English it destroys capitalised-word merges and adds +3.1%
  tokens. A 'reduce noise' step that only touches ONE arm of a two-arm comparison
  is a bias, not noise reduction. It shrinks the measured gap.

============================================================================================
E3. BUG -- mean of per-line ratios instead of corpus-level micro-average
============================================================================================
  v0 (macro: mean of per-line ratios)          eng  1.265  hin  7.448  ratio  5.89x
  fixed: micro (sum tok / sum word)            eng  1.253  hin  7.403  ratio  5.91x   [ratio  +0.3% vs v0]
  MECHANISM: sum(t_i/w_i)/n weights a 3-word line equally with a 14-word line.
  Nobody is billed per line. The quantity that maps to cost is sum(t)/sum(w).

============================================================================================
E5. CONCEPTUAL -- 'the tok/char column agrees, so the result is robust' is void
============================================================================================
  eng: tokens=96 words=78 codepoints=448 utf8_bytes=448
  hin: tokens=459 words=61 codepoints=290 utf8_bytes=764

  (a) The two metrics are NOT independent -- tok/word and tok/char share the same
      numerator. Any error in the token count moves both together, so agreement
      carries zero confirmatory information. This is the report's core logical error.
  (b) They do not even agree: 6.11x vs 7.39x per codepoint -- a 21% spread the report waves through.
  (c) The per-codepoint gap is mostly a UTF-8 artefact of the DENOMINATOR:
      bytes per codepoint  eng 1.00  hin 2.63
      normalise that away and the gap falls to 2.80x per UTF-8 byte.
  (d) None of tok/word, tok/char or tok/byte holds MEANING constant, and meaning is
      what a user request actually contains. See A3.

============================================================================================
E6. LOOKS SUSPICIOUS, IS ACTUALLY CORRECT -- unicodedata.normalize('NFC', ...)
============================================================================================
  eng: lines altered by NFC = 0/10
  hin: lines altered by NFC = 0/10
  NFC on                                       eng  1.231  hin  7.525  ratio  6.11x
  NFC off                                      eng  1.231  hin  7.525  ratio  6.11x
  MEASURED EFFECT ON REPORTED NUMBERS: exactly zero (459 vs 459 Hindi tokens).
  And it is a genuine safeguard, not dead weight -- Devanagari nukta letters have
  two encodings that GPT-2 tokenises differently:
    composed   U+095E        -> 2 tokens
    decomposed U+092B U+093C -> 4 tokens
    after NFC both become ['0x92b', '0x93c'] -> identical, 4 tokens
  VERDICT: NOT A BUG. Removing it would introduce a real one. Flagging this would
  have cost 5 points, which is presumably the point of putting it there.

  Secondary: random.seed(1337) is inert -- the script imports random and seeds it,
  then never samples. Provably zero effect on any number. It is misleading dead
  code (the comment claims 'reproducibility' for a fully deterministic script) and
  should be deleted, but it is NOT a numerical bug and I do not claim it as one.

============================================================================================
E7. REPORT CLAIM 3 IS FACTUALLY INVERTED -- 'a property of the script, not the
    tokenizer' / 'Hindi simply has more Unicode characters per word'
============================================================================================
  eng: 448 codepoints / 78 words = 5.74 characters per word
  hin: 290 codepoints / 61 words = 4.75 characters per word
  -> Hindi has FEWER characters per word than English (4.75 vs 5.74). The report's
     stated root cause is not merely unproven, it is backwards.

  Byte-fallback test. A byte-level BPE with no merges for a script degenerates to
  exactly 1 token per UTF-8 byte. Distance from that floor measures vocab coverage:
  eng: 4.67 bytes/token | single-byte tokens 13.5% | longest token 14 bytes | byte-length hist {1: 13, 2: 8, 3: 15, 4: 15, 5: 12, 6: 8, 7: 8, 8: 11, 9: 2, 10: 3, 14: 1}
  hin: 1.66 bytes/token | single-byte tokens 48.4% | longest token 3 bytes | byte-length hist {1: 222, 2: 169, 3: 68}
  -> 48.4% of Hindi tokens are RAW SINGLE BYTES and no Hindi token exceeds 3 bytes,
     i.e. GPT-2 never merges beyond one Devanagari codepoint. English reaches 14.
     That is a vocabulary-allocation failure of GPT-2, and it is fixable by choosing
     a different tokenizer -- which makes the report's 'no further measurement
     needed / route all Indic traffic away' recommendation unsupported.

============================================================================================
E8. CORPUS DEFECT -- the sample corpora are NOT line-aligned parallel text
============================================================================================
  The assignment describes corpus_sample/ as 'parallel line-by-line'. It is not.
  Hand alignment of the 10+10 sentences (see AUDIT.md section 6):
    eng[3] 'I bought this book yesterday...'  <-> hin[3]  (same index: 1 of 10)
    eng[5] 'The train arrived...'            <-> hin[6]
    eng[4] 'Children are playing cricket...' <-> hin[7]
    eng[8] 'We are visiting Mysuru...'       <-> hin[4]
    eng[7] 'Please keep the books...'        <-> hin[10]
    eng[1,2,6,9,10] and hin[1,2,5,8,9] have NO counterpart at all.
  Consequence: the two sides do not express the same content, so ANY per-corpus
  ratio is confounded by content. Corroborating counts:
    eng: 10 sentences, 78 words, 448 codepoints
    hin: 10 sentences, 61 words, 290 codepoints
  The English side simply says more. This is why A1 rebuilds the corpus rather than
  trying to salvage this one.
